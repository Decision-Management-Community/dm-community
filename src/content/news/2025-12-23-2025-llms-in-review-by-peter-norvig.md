<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/pnorvig/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Peter Norvig</a> wrote today: &#8220;<em>I am now done comparing three LLMs to my own coding on the Advent of Code problems. <strong>The LLMs did great!</strong> They couldn&#8217;t have done it last year.&#8221; </em> Here are his main conclusions after asking 3 different LLMs to solve 12 puzzles:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><em>Overall, the LLMs did <strong>very well</strong>, producing code that gives the correct answer to every puzzle.</em></li>



<li><em>I&#8217;m beginning to think I should use an LLM as an assistant for all my coding, not just as an experiment like this.</em></li>



<li><em>This is a huge improvement over just one year ago, when LLMs could not perform anywhere near this level.</em></li>



<li><em>The three LLMS seemed to be roughly equal in quality.</em></li>



<li><em>The LLMs knew the things you would want an experienced software engineer to know</em></li>
</ul>
