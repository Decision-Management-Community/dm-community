<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="ember66">Pieter van Schalkwyk posted on LinkedIn &#8220;<a href="https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/when-ai-agents-tell-you-what-want-hear-sycophancy-van-schalkwyk-yonec/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">When AI Agents Tell You What You Want to Hear: The Sycophancy Problem</a>&#8220;. In particular, he says: &#8220;Modern AI models learn to maximize user satisfaction metrics. This training creates a fundamental bias toward telling people what they want to hear. When businesses deploy single AI systems, this presents manageable risks. <em>The problem explodes when multiple AI agents collaborate. Each agent&#8217;s tendency to agree reinforces the others, creating false consensus. What looks like unanimous support often masks critical flaws that no agent dares to surface.</em><br> Consider a simple scenario: five AI agents evaluating a risky investment. If each agent has a 30% chance of providing agreeable rather than accurate analysis, the probability of getting genuine dissent drops to near zero. The math is unforgiving.&#8221;</p>
