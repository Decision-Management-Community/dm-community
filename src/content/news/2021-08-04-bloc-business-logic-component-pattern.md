<p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="a980">Initially Business Logic Component (BLoC) pattern was introduced by Google as a solution to handle states in <a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://medium.com/flutter-community/flutter-bloc-pattern-for-dummies-like-me-c22d40f05a56" target="_blank">Flutter</a> applications. It allows you to reduce the workload on UI components by separating the business logic from them. Over time, other frameworks also started to support the BLoC pattern &#8211; see e.g. how to use the BLoC pattern with <a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://blog.bitsrc.io/using-bloc-pattern-with-react-cb6fdcfa623b" target="_blank">React</a>. It brings many benefits of business logic externalization: Flexibility to update application logic, Reuse logic, Ease of testing, Scalability. It is only natural to implement the BLoC pattern using a rule engine and additionally move control over business logic from programmers to subject matter experts. <a rel="noreferrer noopener" href="https://blog.bitsrc.io/using-bloc-pattern-with-react-cb6fdcfa623b" target="_blank">Link</a></p>
