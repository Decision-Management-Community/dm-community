<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/sdalbera/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Stéphane Dalbera</a> wrote: <em>We should stop using “over-engineering” as a catch-all buzzword and as a convenient disguise for defending mediocrity, quick-and-dirty practices, and the refusal to develop deeper expertise.</em> </p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>Reducing the entire toolbox of software engineering to what a beginner fresh out of a bootcamp can understand is not a fight against over-engineering. It is an endorsement of intellectual laziness and the gradual impoverishment of the craft.</em></p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>And in the end, I have seen far more disasters caused by under-engineering, or by the complete absence of engineering altogether, than by actual over-engineering</em>. <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/sdalbera_lets-talk-about-over-engineering-i-often-share-7464039702841544704-Kz6T/?utm_source=share&amp;utm_medium=member_desktop&amp;rcm=ACoAAAAFIMoBtgF6zELsOiwFm0hGYH1dIteE6pQ" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Link</a></p>
