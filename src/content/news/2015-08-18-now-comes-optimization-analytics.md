<p>Along with descriptive, diagnostic, predictive and prescriptive analytics a new term &#8220;Optimization Analytics&#8221; was proposed in the just published article &#8220;<a href="http://robertkugel.ventanaresearch.com/2015/08/13/optimization-analytics-comes-to-the-mass-market/" target="_blank">Optimization Analytics Comes to the Mass Market</a>&#8221; by Robert Kugel.  Here are a few extracts: <!--more--></p>
<blockquote><p>&#8220;We’re now on the cusp of “democratizing” what I call <strong>optimization analytics</strong>. Big data technologies are making it feasible and affordable for even midsize companies to work with much larger data sets than they have been able to in the past.&#8221;</p>
<p>&#8220;Optimization analytics is a breakthrough technology with the potential to improve business performance and create a competitive advantage. <strong>You can’t do optimization in your head</strong>, and it’s not feasible in desktop spreadsheets for anything but the most basic use cases, such as linear programming optimizations on relatively small data sets. This is a good reason for almost any company to consider adopting optimization software.&#8221;</p>
<p>&#8220;From a practical standpoint, though, the value of these large data sets can only be realized by moving optimization analytics out of the exclusive realm of data scientists and <strong>into the hands of business analysts</strong>. These analysts are the ones who have a sufficient understanding of the business and the subtleties of the data to find useful and repeatable optimization opportunities.&#8221;</p></blockquote>
