<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><a href="https://www.linkedin.com/company/fermatslibrary/posts/">Fermat&#8217;s Library</a> shares interesting facts on LinkedIn. Here is <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/fermatslibrary_fun-fact-in-the-1780s-a-german-schoolteacher-activity-7297985390324387842-heO-?utm_source=share&amp;utm_medium=member_desktop&amp;rcm=ACoAAAAFIMoBtgF6zELsOiwFm0hGYH1dIteE6pQ" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">one of them</a>. In the 1780s a German schoolteacher gave his 8-year-olds a problem to keep them busy. He asked them to add up all the numbers from 1 to 100: 1 + 2 + 3 + &#8230; + 98 + 99 + 100 = ?<br><br>One student came up with the answer in just 2 minutes. The boy told the teacher that he had just “folded” the numbers so that 1 joins with 100, 2 joins with 99&#8230; Each pair of numbers added up to 101. He counted 50 pairs of numbers and so he multiplied 50 by 101 which gave him the answer 5050.<br><br>That boy’s name was <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_Gauss" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Carl Friedrich Gauss</a>.</p>
