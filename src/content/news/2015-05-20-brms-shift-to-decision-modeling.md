<p>More and more BR vendors these days use the term B<strong>D</strong>RMS instead of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_rule_management_system" target="_blank">BRMS</a>. Recently FICO rebranded its flagship BRMS solution to &#8220;<strong>D</strong>ecision <strong>R</strong>ules <strong>M</strong>anagement <strong>S</strong>ystem&#8221; (replacing “business” with “decision”).  Even the major annual BR event now is called &#8220;<a href="http://www.businessrulesforum.com/" target="_blank">Business Rules and Decisions Forum</a>&#8220;. Read the interesting <a href="http://www.fico.com/en/blogs/analytics-optimization/recall-business-rules-management-rage/" target="_blank">article</a> <!--more-->that starts with the question:</p>
<blockquote><p>Does anyone remember when the term “<a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_rule_management_system" target="_blank">Business Rule Management Systems</a>” (or BRMS) was coined?</p></blockquote>
<p>Here are a few more interesting quotes:</p>
<blockquote><p>BRMS focused on rules but not necessarily other critical facets of decision management, such as predictive or prescriptive modeling, or optimization (solving large, complex business problems using mathematical solvers).</p>
<p>By shifting the focus from rules to decisions, <strong>Decision Modeling</strong> adds the necessary structure that was missing in the business rules approach, without letting go of its benefits: rules are still declarative, modular and independent, but they only have meaning in the context of a decision.</p></blockquote>
