<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Stefaan Lambrecht from TRIPOD shared a typical story for insurance operations. Embedding critical business logic within a script inside a process cost the insurer €50 million. This wasn’t caused by careless people or bad intentions. It happened because the decision was invisibly embedded in a process where no one thought to look. That situation is far more common than most insurers realize. The natural conclusion is in the title. <a href="https://stefaanlambrecht.substack.com/p/the-insurance-brain" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Link</a></p>



<!--more-->



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;During a modernization program at a large insurance company, the CIO asked what seemed like a simple question:</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">“Where exactly do we calculate the deductible for travel claims?”</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It should have been easy to answer.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But the team couldn’t find it.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">They searched the claims system.<br>They checked the policy administration platform.<br>They looked in the rule repository.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Nothing.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Eventually they discovered the rule buried inside a workflow task—hidden in about <strong>400 lines of script code</strong> inside a process.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">But the real shock came later.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That rule had <strong>not been updated for four years</strong>, even though the underwriting department had already changed the deductible policy.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">As a result, the claims system had been applying the wrong deductible.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">For four years.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">When they calculated the financial impact, the numbers were staggering.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">That one hidden rule had resulted in <strong>over €50 million in incorrect claim payments</strong>.&#8221;</p>
