<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Dr.&nbsp;<a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/ACoAAAC7sxwBytvXDhXbbTCbku4AukgIXgVgqAo">Walid Saba</a>&nbsp;makes a compelling case for combining&nbsp;<a href="https://www.linkedin.com/feed/hashtag/?keywords=symbolic&amp;highlightedUpdateUrns=urn%3Ali%3Aactivity%3A7088308674531594240">#symbolic</a>&nbsp;<a href="https://www.linkedin.com/feed/hashtag/?keywords=ai&amp;highlightedUpdateUrns=urn%3Ali%3Aactivity%3A7088308674531594240">#AI</a>&nbsp;with the strengths of large language models. The limitations of current&nbsp;<a href="https://www.linkedin.com/feed/hashtag/?keywords=llms&amp;highlightedUpdateUrns=urn%3Ali%3Aactivity%3A7088308674531594240">#LLMs</a>&nbsp;are well articulated, especially the lack of explainability and failures in intentional contexts. Moving to a symbolic system could address these issues. &nbsp;His <a href="https://lnkd.in/dS8CHqZR" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">paper</a> makes a compelling argument and outlines an original approach to move toward symbolic,&nbsp;<a href="https://www.linkedin.com/feed/hashtag/?keywords=explainable&amp;highlightedUpdateUrns=urn%3Ali%3Aactivity%3A7088308674531594240">#explainable</a>&nbsp;LLMs. Expanding the reverse engineering analysis and testing the ideas on broader linguistic phenomena would be interesting next steps. The vision of combining strengths of modern AI with symbolic&nbsp;<a href="https://www.linkedin.com/feed/hashtag/?keywords=representations&amp;highlightedUpdateUrns=urn%3Ali%3Aactivity%3A7088308674531594240">#representations</a>&nbsp;is thought-provoking. <a href="https://lnkd.in/dS8CHqZR" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">Link</a></p>
