<p>On Dec. 18 Bill Gates <a href="https://www.gatesnotes.com/About-Bill-Gates/Codescope?WT.mc_id=20181221144700_Codescope_BG-TW&amp;WT.tsrc=BGTW&amp;linkId=61391021" target="_blank" rel="noopener">wrote</a>: &#8220;<em>I’ve been fascinated by the artist and inventor Leonardo da Vinci for decades. He had one of the most innovative minds ever. Next year is the 500<sup>th</sup> anniversary of his death, and I thought I would share a short video about a project I’ve worked on that is helping to mark the occasion. </em><em>The project is called the Codescope. It’s an interactive kiosk with a touch screen that lets you explore the Codex Leicester, a notebook of Leonardo’s that I bought in 1994. Using the Codescope, you can learn about the history of the notebook, see every page of Leonardo’s original writing, get a translation, and even watch animated versions of his drawings.</em>&#8221; <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zwGdnCNi8Ss&amp;feature=youtu.be" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Video</a><!--more--> The Codescope allows visitors to study details of Leonardo&#8217;s drawings, reverse his Italian mirror script, and read the manuscript in modern English or Italian. It also shows users how each section of a page is related to other information, giving an inside look into how Leonardo&#8217;s mind worked.</p>
