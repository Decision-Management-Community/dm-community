<p>NPR just published an <a href="https://npr.org/2018/07/26/632724239/facial-recognition-software-wrongly-identifies-28-lawmakers-as-crime-suspects" target="_blank" rel="noopener">article</a> that says: &#8220;<em><strong>Facial recognition software sold by Amazon mistakenly identified 28 members of Congress as people who had been arrested for crimes</strong>, the American Civil Liberties Union announced on Thursday. Amazon Rekognition has been marketed as tool that provides extremely accurate facial analysis through photos and video</em>.&#8221; <a href="https://linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:6428591927888797696/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Fred Simkin</a> posted the following comment: <!--more--></p>
<p><em>Decisions require judgment. Judgment is a product of &#8220;knowledge&#8221; not just more &#8220;data&#8221;. The piece is an excellent example of the <strong>fallacy of the data only approach</strong> implicit in DL and ML. NN based solutions are excellent filters in hybrid applications, but decisions and actions must be the purview of a knowledge based representation schema component.</em></p>
