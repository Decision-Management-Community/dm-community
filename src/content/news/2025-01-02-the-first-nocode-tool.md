<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Forty-five years ago, Dan Bricklin and Bob Frankston created what is undoubtedly one of the first 𝐍𝐨𝐂𝐨𝐝𝐞 tools, profoundly changing the way data is processed on a computer: the 𝐬𝐩𝐫𝐞𝐚𝐝𝐬𝐡𝐞𝐞𝐭.<br><br>As is often the case with brilliant ideas, their innovation seemed obvious in hindsight, but its impact was extraordinary.<br><br>Previously, even the simplest data processing operations required some programming knowledge.<br><br>The 𝐬𝐩𝐫𝐞𝐚𝐝𝐬𝐡𝐞𝐞𝐭, however, empowered countless individuals with <strong>the ability to create problem-solving tools without needing to write a single line of code</strong>.<br><br>𝐕𝐢𝐬𝐢𝐂𝐚𝐥𝐜 is one of the earliest examples of metaphor-driven user interface design, resembling paper spreadsheets.<br><br>This metaphor made the program intuitive and familiar to accountants, economists, and bookkeepers who were not accustomed to using computers.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">𝐕𝐢𝐬𝐢𝐂𝐚𝐥𝐜 release marked a pivotal moment when personal computers transitioned from a hobbyist obsession to a compelling business tool.<br><br>Absolutely all the 𝐬𝐩𝐫𝐞𝐚𝐝𝐬𝐡𝐞𝐞𝐭𝐬 you use today are inherited from this innovation, which remains by far the most widely used 𝐍𝐨𝐂𝐨𝐝𝐞 product.</p>
