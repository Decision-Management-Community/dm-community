Here are quotes from his latest <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/sdalbera_mytribute2thepeers-activity-7299303215488012288-3Sbg?utm_source=share&amp;utm_medium=member_desktop&amp;rcm=ACoAAAAFIMoBtgF6zELsOiwFm0hGYH1dIteE6pQ" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">post</a> about Marvin Minsky with comments by <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/philippekahn/">Philippe Kahn</a>: &#8220;<em>Minsky helped develop early AI systems for solving mathematical problems, playing chess, and simulating visual perception. He focused on creating machines that could replicate human thought but remained skeptical of the idea that machines could quickly achieve true intelligence&#8230; In The Society of Mind (1986), Minsky expanded his theory that intelligence emerges from the interactions of simple processes, or &#8220;agents,&#8221; each responsible for a different aspect of thought.<br><br>In the 1980s and 1990s, Minsky remained a leading figure in AI, advocating for interdisciplinary approaches to understanding intelligence. </em></p>
