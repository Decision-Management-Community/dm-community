John Brandon Elam refuses the one-sentence definition of a policy and starts at a car lot instead. The buyers who do well under pressure are the ones who set their ceilings, tradeoffs, and hard nos while they were still calm — before the salesman, the paperwork, and the new-car smell. That pre-built rule, executed when the moment arrives, is a policy; it's also the premise behind building decisions once under good conditions and letting them run under bad ones.
