Paul insists that &#8220;<em>It’s time to trade rule technology dating back to the 80’s for state of the art AI</em>&#8221; and points that &#8220;<em>BRMS are incapable of the functionality people naturally expect (e.g., robust inference)</em>&#8220;. James Taylor <a href="https://twitter.com/Jacob_OpenRules/status/983332716392443905" target="_blank" rel="noopener">insists</a> that &#8220;<em>People don&#8217;t expect robust inference, experts do 🙂 DMN coordinates business rules/tabular decision logic, predictive analytics, AI&#8230; and it seems to be working for 100% of problems</em>&#8220;. Jacob Feldman <a href="https://openrules.wordpress.com/2018/03/16/decision-management-and-semantic-reasoning/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">wrote</a> about importance of the integration of decision management and semantic reasoning.<!--more--></p>
<p>The current version of DMN does not require an execution engine to have inferential capabilities that allowed several BPM vendors to quickly add DMN implementations. More than that, DMN requires a user to build DRDs that Paul&#8217;s used to call &#8220;flow-charts&#8221; and wrote about them:  &#8220;<em>Visual programming may be a nice and useful capability, but the benefits of artificial intelligence technology hardly accrue from dressing up a procedural  language!</em>&#8221;  These issues will be among hot discussion topics at the upcoming <a href="https://decisioncamp2018.wordpress.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">DecisionCAMP</a> and <a href="https://ruleml2018.gforge.uni.lu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">RuleML+RR</a> conference. We invite business rules and semantic web practitioners to provide their comments right here.</p>
